The particle size windows

PM10, PM2.5, the coarse band between them and dust of no stated size are one family of measurements cut at different sizes, spelled seventeen ways by five lists. The window each row was cut at is a curated field now, so a row lands on the window it states — and a row nobody has read stops the merge rather than minting a substance.

What a particle flow says

A particle flow says how much, where it went, and which particles were counted. The first two are the unit and the context. The third is the window — a pair of aerodynamic-diameter bounds in micrometres — and it is the identity: two spellings of one window are one thing, and two windows are two things however alike they are spelled.

The scheme

Seven windows, ours. No published vocabulary carries a particle size band, so nothing here cites one; the bounds are what a reviewer checks, and a window's parent is the smallest window whose bounds contain it. Every window is always published under its Particulates spelling as well as its Particles (…) name, so a search for the ecoinvent-2 form finds it whichever vendor's rows reached it.

What each method says, and what this list carries

Per window, the consensus implementation's factors in the particulate categories: how many are published, how many of those were carried rather than stated, and the number in unspecified air. A window nobody characterises takes its nearest broader window's stated number inside the compartment — the coarse band takes PM10's, the ultrafine cut takes PM2.5's, the above-ten fraction takes the size-unstated total's — and the compartment convention then spreads it into the outdoor strata. Under EF 3.1 nothing characterises the total or anything above it, so particles above ten micrometres stay blank there.
